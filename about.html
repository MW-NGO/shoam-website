About us

Education is for all. So why wait?

Malawi is not short of talented students. It is short of places where young people, parents and schools can talk honestly about fees, books, transport and distance — and get practical help. This is who we are and what we are building.

Student-led By students in Malawi, for students in Malawi

Our story

A country full of promise, and students tired of being a statistic.

Malawi has students with ideas, energy and ambition in every village, town and school. What they often lack is the money, materials, information and steady support that turn those intentions into a future finished well.

SHOAM — the Student Helping Organization of Malawi — began with a public question from its founder, Frank Mkhalipi: do you know SHOAM? The call went out to students across the nation, in English and in Chichewa — to classrooms, families and communities, not only campuses.

The answer is still being written. We are building the organisation in the open, starting with the simple promise on our page: education is for all. If you want to help write the next chapter, you are invited in.

What we are for

Short enough to remember. Serious enough to be held to.

Our mission

To help students across Malawi stay in school, get the materials and information they need, and find a local community that takes their ambitions seriously.

Our vision

A Malawi where education really is for all — where a student's future is not decided by their village, their family income or the cost of getting to school.

Our values

  • Education for all — the motto is the whole point.
  • Student-led — Malawian students are the organisers, not the audience.

  • Practical help — fees, books, transport and information before speeches.
  • Honesty — we say what is done and what is still planned.

What the vision means in practice

A motto is only useful if it changes what you do on Monday.

“Education is for all” is not a feeling. It looks like school fees paid before a child is sent home. It looks like a notebook and a bus fare to an exam centre, a subject explained before the choice is made, and a student still turning up when the term gets hard.

So we measure ourselves on things we can actually see: materials delivered, students reached, questions answered, records kept, and whether the people we serve would tell us to keep going.

The challenge

What we are actually up against

We are not here to describe students as a problem. We are here to name the barriers around them — and then take them apart, one at a time.

The cost of simply turning up

For many families, school fees, transport, uniforms and exam costs add up to a decision no child should have to make. Help that arrives early changes the whole term.

Learning materials are not evenly shared

Some students have books, notebooks and a quiet place to read. Others share one exercise book with three people. Closing that gap is basic, not luxurious.

Distance decides too much

A student who lives far from a secondary school or a training centre faces time, cost and safety barriers that are invisible on a map.

Girls face extra barriers

Early pregnancy, period poverty, safety concerns and household expectations still push girls out of school. Support has to reach them before they leave.

Information arrives too late

Many students choose subjects, courses and careers with almost no honest guidance. A short conversation at the right time can change a path.

No one knows where to start

Good intentions are everywhere. What is missing is a clear place to plug in — a group that will tell you what is needed and what you can actually do.

Our commitment on data. We will only publish numbers we can stand behind — deliveries, attendance, member counts. Where we do not yet have a verified figure, we say so rather than borrowing one.

How we work

Listen. Share. Support. Follow up.

Four steps, repeated honestly. No programme is finished when the materials are handed over.

Listen

We ask students, parents and schools what is actually blocking them — fees, books, transport, uniforms or guidance — rather than assuming we know.

Share

We put the need in front of the people who can help — students, alumni, businesses and well-wishers across Malawi.

Support

School fees where we can, notebooks, transport, mentoring and introductions. Practical things with names and dates attached.

Follow up

We check what happened, write it down honestly, and let supporters see where their help went.

The story in our logo

An open book. A heart. A way forward.

  • The open book. Education is for all — the page is open to every student, not just a few.
  • The heart. It is the centre of the SHOAM logo: care for students, and a promise that education belongs to all of them.
  • The upward path. Small, practical steps turn a question into a plan and a plan into progress.
  • Green and teal. Growth and learning — the colours of a classroom where no one is left behind.
